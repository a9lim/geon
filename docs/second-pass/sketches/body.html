Proposal for the sim overhaul’s second pass · 24 October 2026

A plate of spacetime

Worldline was the overhaul’s pilot, and the three sims that followed went further. Lumen, Miasma and Shoals are now dense, labelled plates, each with a key, a scale and a numbered figure. Worldline is still grey disks on graph paper and a wireframe box. This pass would make it the most finished of the four: bodies that say what they are, light that shows how far it has travelled, fields that read like a survey sheet, and a spacetime view drawn like a figure from a relativity textbook. Every figure below is a live sketch with a Today and a Proposed drawing of the same scene.

Where it stands after a pass through all 24 presets

I ran every preset in plane and spacetime views, in both themes and at phone width, and set Worldline beside its siblings. The spacetime view is the best thing on the page: in lab coordinates with a boost set, the observer’s tilted present cutting the world lines is a picture no textbook animates. Around it the scene says little. In most presets the eye finds identical grey disks, thick smudged trails, and a caption that repeats the preset’s description.

What the scene doesn’t say
Which bodies are chargedCharge tints a body |q|/5 of the way from graphite to its ink, so a unit charge is 20% tinted. Cyclotron’s “charges of both signs” look identical.
Which body is selectedNothing marks it in the plane view, though the caption calls it “the red body”. Only its world line turns red once tilted.
Where the light isPhotons are 2-pixel dots. Bremsstrahlung and Hawking evaporation don’t visibly show their own subject.
What the fields are doingThe axion draws as a pixel maze, the Higgs presets show no Higgs field, and a uniform B fills the screen with ⊗.
What anything is calledNo labels, no key, no scale. The siblings have all three.

What already works

The colophon, the caption bar, the margin on the site’s paper, the inks, the Plane and Spacetime tilt, the boost, the proper-time beads on world lines, and the lensed star field in the microlensing preset. None of it changes; the proposal builds on it.

Why it lags

As the pilot, Worldline settled the shared chrome and the 3D camera. Its siblings then spent their effort on the scene itself. This pass gives Worldline that same effort.

Bodies that say what they are and trails that are world lines

Each body becomes a small glyph, drawn like the molecules in Lumen: a lighter body inside a darker outline. Charge gets its full ink at any nonzero value, plus a + or − mark, so the sign survives in grey. Antimatter is hollow, the hole in Dirac’s sea. A black hole becomes the darkest thing on the page in both themes, with its ergosphere dashed at 2M. The gap between the dashed ring and the horizon at r₊ then reads the spin directly. A tick on the rim turns with proper time.

The trail stops being a separate drawing. It becomes the plane view of the world line: a hairline with a bead every equal step of the body’s own time. Then the plane view is the spacetime view seen from straight above, as AGENTS.md already says it is, and the tilt raises the trails into world lines instead of fading one drawing out and another in.

Build

In particle.wgsl: an antialiased edge from fwidth in place of the hard discard, the outline, the sign mark and the hollow fill, all in the rest frame, so they contract with the disk. The black hole takes a void ink instead of the text colour. Trails come from the world-line ring through spacetime-render.wgsl’s line pipeline at el = π/2, which retires trail-render.wgsl; the ring holds 128 time units, more than any trail needs. Selection gets a ring in ring-render.wgsl.

What it fixes

The charge tint, the white black hole in the dark theme, its faceted trail, the unmarked selection, and the jagged disk edges at a pixel ratio of 1.

Light you can see kinked field lines, photons as wave packets

Worldline already gives every charge its full Liénard–Wiechert field, so the radiation is there in the forces, but nothing on the canvas shows it. Purcell’s textbook drawing shows it in one picture. When a charge changes course, its field lines bend on a circle that grows at c. Outside the circle the field still points to where the charge would be had nothing happened; inside, it points to where the charge is. The bend is the radiation. Drawing the field lines from the charges would put that picture on the canvas wherever a charge accelerates. In the cyclotron preset Worldline opens on, every charge would trail a spiral of kinks, one turn per orbit.

The photons ride those circles. Each one becomes a short wave packet, so its wavelength reads its energy and its ink reads its Doppler shift. A blueshifted photon is indigo with tight wiggles, and a redshifted one is sienna and loose.

Build

The heatmap grid already evaluates the Liénard–Wiechert E to get Bz. Read it back every few frames, trace lines on the CPU from seeds around each charge (proportional to |q|), and draw them on the overlay canvas, which already draws through the tilt. That reuses this sketch’s code. Photons become oriented quads in boson-render.wgsl, with the wave drawn in the fragment shader.

To measure first

The grid’s resolution sets how sharp a kink can be. At 128² over the view a cell is about 10 px, which should hold, but bremsstrahlung’s kinks are thin. If they blur, lines near a recent acceleration can be traced through the exact field instead.

The spacetime view as a plate a cone that reads as a cone, named events, a key

Tilted, Worldline is already a spacetime diagram. It can be drawn with the conventions of one. The past light cone gets its two silhouette lines and dashed back arcs, so it reads as a solid. Lines under the sheet of the present are seen through it, lighter, so the sheet reads as a surface. Events are marked: departures, turnarounds, reunions, emissions and absorptions. The bodies that matter are named on the plate with their proper times. A key lists what the scene contains, a dimension line along the plane’s front edge gives the scale of space, and the time axis gives the scale of time.

The twin paradox is the test case, with light pulses passing between the twins. It is Bondi’s argument made visible. Home’s pulses reach the traveller every eight of its time on the way out and every two on the way back, and the beads and circles on the red world line count it out.

Build

The cone in vs_cone splits each ring at the silhouette angles and dashes the back half; the generators are two more strips. Hidden-line lightening is a test against the projected plane in vs_line. Events come from what the sim already reports: merge results, deaths, boson emission and absorption (emitterId), and per-preset marks such as the twin presets’ turnaround. The overlay canvas draws the labels, the key and the dimension line.

Also

Observer coordinates refit the camera to the sheared scene, which today drifts off the top and right of the frame. Pion exchange, drawn this way, becomes a Feynman diagram in real space: nucleon world lines with pion lines between them, each beaded by its own clock.

Fields drawn like an atlas contours, field lines, symbols, hatching

The Fields overlay today is a texture: a wash by sign, line-integral noise combed along the field, and stippled ⊙ and ⊗. It reads as weather. These drawings are what a survey office or a physics text would print. Potentials are contours, with index contours heavier and labelled and gravity’s wells ticked downhill like the depression contours on a map. The electric field becomes field lines, with equipotentials solid where positive and dashed where negative. The magnetic field stays ⊙ and ⊗ on an octave lattice that opens up where the field is weak. The scalar fields are hatched by vacuum, with the walls drawn as contours. Every field stays inside the domain.

Build

Contours, ticks and labels come from a grid readback on the overlay canvas, as in Fig. 2: marching squares on the 128² grid every few frames costs about a millisecond. The ⊙ ⊗ octave lattice and the hatching stay in their shaders (heatmap-render, field-render), with thresholds per octave and both signs hatched.

The axion maze

field-render.wgsl draws an axion contour every 0.05 whatever the field’s range. Grid-scale noise far above 0.05 turns every cell into a contour, and the hatch flips sign cell by cell. An interval set from the field’s RMS, and a sign read from the smoothed value, fix it.

Around the scene caption, key, margin

  • A numbered figure with live values, in every preset. Lumen and Miasma caption “Fig. 1.” and state what you are looking at with its numbers. Worldline does this for a few presets; most repeat their description. Each of the 24 presets gets a caption builder. Kepler’s, for example, would give the planets’ periods and how well energy is holding.
  • A key in the scene’s corner. As in Fig. 1, listing only what is present and which overlays are on. It hides with the grid (G).
  • A scale bar in the plane view, snapping to 1, 2 and 5. The dimension line and time axis take over when tilted.
  • One red. Force arrows and clock arcs use the accent today. Arrows move to the text ink as hairlines and clock arcs to the body’s ink, so red means the selection and nothing else.
  • One ink per force, in the margin and the scene. Gravity’s switch is umber, but its potential is drawn in graphite. Relativity and 1PN share sienna with positive charge. The Forces section becomes the legend: each row shows its ink and its line pattern beside its switch, as Lumen’s Lines list does.
  • Lighter switches. A switch that is on gets a thick black outline today. It becomes a hairline with an ink fill.
  • One row on a phone. At 390 px the top-right controls wrap to a second row over the scene. Speed and language fold into the Controls sheet.
  • Labels on the bodies where a preset names them: the nucleus, the home twin, the traveller, the black hole with its spin. Each preset authors a few, placed with the collision check Miasma uses for its isochrones.

Order of work

  1. Fixes first. The axion interval, the plane-view selection, the void ink, the observer refit, expansion and misalignment when tilted, fields kept to the domain, red only for the selection, the phone bar. Each is small and stands alone.
  2. Bodies and trails (Fig. 1). The glyph shader, trails drawn from the world-line ring, the selection ring.
  3. The plate’s furniture. Live captions for all 24 presets, the key, the scale bar, and per-preset labels.
  4. The spacetime plate (Fig. 3). The cone’s silhouette and back arcs, events, lines lightened under the sheet.
  5. Light (Fig. 2). Photon wave packets first, then field lines from the grid readback.
  6. The atlas (Fig. 4). Contours with ticks and labels, the octave lattice, hatched vacua.
  7. The margin as a legend, lighter switches, one ink per force.

After step 1 some of this can run in parallel lanes, as the first round did, but lanes have to split by file: steps 2 and 4 share spacetime-render.wgsl, and steps 3, 5 and 6 share the overlay canvas. Every step is drawing only and leaves the physics alone, so the oracle page should pass unchanged; it runs once on the final candidate anyway. Each step ships with light, dark and phone screenshots, a console check and the frame rate on the default preset.

For you to decide

Trails as world lines, always?

Retiring the trail pass makes the tilt continuous and removes a pipeline. Trails would lose their thickness, which some presets (magnetic, nucleus) use to read as tangles. I’d retire it.

Field lines in place of the comb?

Lines read direction and strength and show radiation; the comb reads texture. I’d replace the comb for E and keep it for gravity only if contours alone feel too sparse.

How much labelling per preset?

A few authored labels per preset (24 small additions) or none, only the key. Labels are where Lumen and Miasma feel finished, so I’d author them.

Fix first, or design first?

Step 1 is independent and could ship this week on its own, before you’ve looked at any of the rest.

The sketches are toy models standing in for Worldline’s engine; the drawing is what carries over. Their sources would move to docs/sim-overhaul/ beside the first round’s if this is ratified.
